The question

How do you handle more customers, faster, without the quality of support slipping?

Most teams answer that question with separate tools: one for tickets, a spreadsheet for the roster, another for QC scores, a slide deck for the monthly report. The links between them live in the lead’s head. A low QC score doesn’t reach the training plan until the next review. The forecast doesn’t reach the roster until the queue is already on fire. Atlas builds those links in.

What I built

Eleven modules, four jobs

Channels

AI chatbot
Answers only from help articles and cites them. When it can’t help, it hands over to a person with the transcript.
Email
Incoming emails become tickets; replies thread back and reopen them. Automatic acknowledgement and survey emails.
Workspaces and roles
A private space for each company, with Admin, Team Lead, Agent and QA roles.

The desk

Ticket desk
Queue, assignment, notes, an AI copilot and saved replies with the customer’s details filled in.
SLA tracking
Reply and resolve targets per priority, with a live countdown that pauses while waiting on the customer.
CSAT
A private survey link on every ticket, plus ratings at the end of bot chats.

Quality and people

Quality (QC)
A weighted scorecard. The AI scores first; a person finalises.
Training
Lessons with AI-written quizzes. A low QC score assigns the linked course.
Rostering
Weekly shifts with breaks and overnight cover, and a leave approval flow.

Planning and insight

Capacity planning
An hourly forecast from ticket history, Erlang C staffing with shrinkage, and a heat map of roster gaps.
Reports
Any date range against the period before. Every table downloads as CSV.

How it connects

Three loops a CX lead runs

Content

The bot shows the team what to teach it next

  1. Customer asksThe bot searches published articles.
  2. Bot can’t answerHands over with the transcript and a reason.
  3. Gap is loggedKnowledge Gaps groups it by topic.
  4. Article writtenOne click from the gap, category filled in.
  5. Next customer answeredDeflection rises on that topic.
Quality

Coaching happens when the gap is found

  1. Ticket resolvedThe customer rates it from their survey link.
  2. AI scores itAgainst the team’s weighted scorecard.
  3. Reviewer finalisesAdjusts scores and adds feedback.
  4. Low score foundThe linked course is assigned, due in a week.
  5. Agent passesReports track it next to their QC trend.
Planning

Demand sets the staffing target

  1. Ticket historyAveraged into contacts per hour of the week.
  2. Erlang CAgents needed for the service level, plus shrinkage.
  3. Roster overlayPeople on shift, minus breaks and leave.
  4. Gap heat mapShows exactly which hours are short.
  5. SLA resultsShow whether the fix worked.

Design decisions

Choices a support lead would make

The bot answers only from articles
A confident wrong answer costs more trust than “let me get a person”.
Handovers are grouped by reason
“No article” is a content fix; “needs staff” is a process or tooling fix. They need different owners.
SLA due times are fixed at creation
Changing targets later shouldn’t rewrite last month’s results.
The clock pauses on Waiting on Customer
Agents aren’t penalised for time the customer takes.
Medians next to averages
One ticket stuck for a week drags an average; the median shows the typical customer’s wait.
The AI scores QC, a person finalises it
The AI makes it fast enough to review more conversations, and a person stays accountable for each score. The report tracks how far the AI’s scores differ from the final ones.
Erlang C, not tickets ÷ agents
Queues don’t scale linearly. The standard contact-centre model shows the real headcount.
Only the ticket’s customer can reply onto it
A guessed ticket number in an email subject can’t post into someone else’s case.

Built to be adopted

Free to run, upgraded by configuration

  • The AI runs on Gemini’s free tier, and moving to a paid model is a small code change.
  • Email works end to end in demo mode. Sending real mail needs two environment variables once the company’s domain is verified.
  • Incoming mail arrives through a private webhook for each workspace. It accepts Postmark’s format or simple JSON from any other provider.
  • A test inbox sends pretend customer emails through the same steps as real ones.
