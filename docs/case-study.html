Running support as one system: channels, quality, people and planning

A working, multi-company support platform for a fictional smart-ring brand. It covers what a CX lead runs every day: an AI chatbot and email, SLAs, quality checks, training, rosters, capacity and reporting.

Vishal Misal Next.js · Supabase · Google Gemini Try the customer chat Source on GitHub

The question

How do you handle more customers, faster, without the quality of support slipping?

Most teams answer that question with separate tools: one for tickets, a spreadsheet for the roster, another for QC scores, a slide deck for the monthly report. The links between them live in the lead’s head. A low QC score doesn’t reach the training plan until the next review. The forecast doesn’t reach the roster until someone notices the queue is on fire. I wanted to see what it looks like when those links are built in.

What I built

Eleven modules, one workspace

AI chatbot
Answers only from published help articles and cites them. Hands over to a person with the full transcript when it can’t help.
Email
Incoming emails become tickets that the AI has already read. Replies thread back onto the same ticket and reopen it. Customers get an automatic acknowledgement and a survey link.
Ticket desk
Queue, assignment, internal notes, an AI copilot that drafts replies, and saved replies with the customer’s name filled in.
SLA tracking
First-reply and resolution targets per priority, with live on-track, at-risk and breached labels. The clock pauses while waiting on the customer.
CSAT
A private survey link on every ticket, plus ratings at the end of bot chats. Results show per agent and over time.
Quality (QC)
A weighted scorecard. The AI scores first; a QA analyst finalises. Results by agent and criterion.
Training
Courses with a lesson and an AI-written quiz. A low QC score assigns the linked course automatically.
Rostering
Weekly shifts with breaks and overnight cover. Agents request leave and leads approve it.
Capacity planning
An hourly forecast from ticket history, Erlang C staffing with shrinkage, and a heat map of roster gaps.
Reports
Any date range against the period before: response and resolution times, SLA, CSAT, agents, channels, contact reasons, QC, deflection, training. CSV for every table.
Workspaces and roles
Each company has its own private workspace, with Admin, Team Lead, Agent and QA Analyst roles.

How it connects

Three loops a CX lead runs

Content: the bot shows the team what to teach it

  1. Customer asks

    The bot searches published articles.

  2. Bot can’t answer

    It hands over with the transcript and a reason.

  3. Gap is logged

    The Knowledge Gaps report groups it by topic.

  4. Article written

    One click from the gap, category filled in.

  5. Next customer answered

    Deflection rises on that topic.

Quality: coaching happens when the gap is found

  1. Ticket resolved

    The customer rates it from the survey link.

  2. AI scores it

    Against the team’s own weighted scorecard.

  3. Reviewer finalises

    A person adjusts the scores and adds feedback.

  4. Low score on a criterion

    The linked course is assigned, due in a week.

  5. Agent passes the quiz

    Reports track it alongside their QC trend.

Planning: demand sets the staffing target

  1. Ticket history

    Averaged into contacts per hour of the week.

  2. Erlang C

    Agents needed to hit the service level, plus shrinkage.

  3. Roster overlay

    People on shift, minus breaks and leave.

  4. Gap heat map

    Shows exactly which hours are short.

  5. SLA results

    Show whether the fix worked.

Design decisions

Choices a support lead would make

DecisionWhy
The bot answers only from articlesA confident wrong answer costs more trust than “let me get a person”.
Handovers are grouped by reason“No article” is a content fix; “needs staff” is a process or tooling fix. They need different owners.
SLA due times are fixed at creationChanging targets later shouldn’t rewrite last month’s results.
The clock pauses on Waiting on CustomerAgents aren’t penalised for time the customer takes.
Medians next to averagesOne ticket stuck for a week drags an average; the median shows the typical customer’s wait.
The AI scores QC, a person finalises itThe AI makes it fast enough to review more conversations, and a person stays accountable for each score. The report tracks how far the AI’s scores differ from the final ones.
Erlang C, not tickets ÷ agentsQueues don’t scale linearly; the standard contact-centre model shows the real headcount.
Only the ticket’s customer can reply onto itA guessed ticket number in an email subject can’t post into someone else’s case.
Agents don’t see team reportsAgents see their own results without a leaderboard of their colleagues.

Measurement

What the reports track

MetricWhy it matters
First reply and resolution timeThe customer’s experience of speed, as a median and an average.
SLA met %, breached and at riskWhether the team keeps its promises, and what needs attention right now.
CSAT, from tickets and from bot chatsShows whether faster or automated handling is costing satisfaction.
Bot deflection and handover reasonsThe volume the bot takes off the queue, and what stops it taking more.
Contact reasonsWhat to fix at the source with product and ops, so contacts don’t happen at all.
QC by agent and criterionWhere coaching is needed, and for whom.
Training passed and overdueWhether the coaching actually happened.
Agents needed vs rostered, per hourWhere the next shift change should go.

There is no live customer traffic yet, so there are no real results to report. A one-click demo dataset fills a workspace with 90 days of made-up activity, including a sync-issue spike and agents with different strengths, so every report can be walked through. In a pilot I’d take two weeks as a baseline, then judge success on SLA met % and first reply time improving while CSAT holds steady or rises.

Built to be adopted

Runs at no cost, and upgrades by configuration

  • The AI runs on Gemini’s free tier, and moving to a paid model is a small code change.
  • Email works end to end in demo mode. Sending real mail is two environment variables once the company’s domain is verified.
  • Incoming mail arrives through a private webhook for each workspace. It accepts Postmark’s format, or simple JSON from any other provider.
  • A test inbox in Settings sends pretend customer emails through the same steps as real ones.
