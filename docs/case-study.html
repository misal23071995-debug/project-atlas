Cutting support volume with an AI chatbot, without hurting CSAT

A working support desk for a fictional smart-ring brand, built to test how far self-service AI can go before a customer needs a person.

The question

How do you take repeat questions off agents’ plates without customers feeling stonewalled by a bot?

Most queues are full of questions that already have an answer: my ring won’t sync, how long does the battery last, what’s the return policy. Agents spend their time retyping help articles while customers with real problems wait. A chatbot can absorb that volume, but a bad one creates a different problem: wrong answers, dead ends, and customers who arrive at an agent already angry.

Approach

Three rules for the bot

  • Answer only from the knowledge base. If no article covers the question, say so. A confident wrong answer costs more trust than “let me get a person”.
  • Know when to stop. Hand over when the question isn’t covered, when only staff can act, when the customer asks for a person, or when they’re upset.
  • Never make the customer repeat themselves. Every handover becomes a ticket with the full chat, a one-line reason, and a priority set from sentiment.

The improvement loop

The bot shows the team what to teach it next

  1. Customer asks

    The bot searches published articles for an answer.

  2. Bot can’t answer

    It hands over with the transcript and a reason.

  3. Gap is logged

    The Knowledge Gaps report groups the question by topic.

  4. Agent writes the article

    One click from the gap, category pre-filled.

  5. Next customer is answered

    Deflection rises on that topic.

Why it hands over

This is a demo with no live customer traffic yet, so there are no results to report. In a pilot I’d set a baseline for the first two weeks, then judge success on deflection rising while CSAT for handed-over chats holds steady or improves.

Also in the desk

The agent side

  • Invite-only agent logins; every reply, note and status change records who made it.
  • An AI copilot on each ticket that summarises the issue, reads sentiment and drafts a reply for the agent to edit.
  • Internal notes, assignment to real agents, and a form for logging phone or walk-in requests.
  • A knowledge base editor, so the support team, not engineers, owns what the bot says.

Next

What I’d build next

  • Order lookup inside the bot, to remove the most common staff-action handover.
  • Email channel, so agent replies reach customers and incoming emails become tickets.
  • A weekly gap digest emailed to the CX lead every Monday.
  • SLA timers and escalation rules on tickets.
